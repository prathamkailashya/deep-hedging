\begin{abstract}
Deep hedging~\citep{buehler2019deep} parameterises a hedging
policy as a neural network and minimises a convex risk measure
directly on the residual P\&L. We address three deployment-limiting
weaknesses with three architectures trained under a unified
protocol. \textbf{W-DRO-T} is a causal Transformer whose loss is
the first-order dual of a Wasserstein-$1$ ambiguity
ball~\citep{blanchet2019quantifying}, contributing a Lipschitz
gradient-norm penalty that vanishes at inference. \textbf{3SCH} is
a three-stage curriculum LSTM that interpolates between
$\cvar_{0.95}$ and the entropic risk along a continuous homotopy
whose regularity follows from Berge's maximum theorem. \textbf{RSE}
is a regime-switching ensemble that gates frozen LSTM, Transformer
and signature experts through a learnable affinity matrix indexed
by realised-volatility, trend, momentum and jump statistics. RSE
attains the lowest in-distribution tail risk on a ten-seed Heston
benchmark ($\cvar_{95}=3.109\pm 0.010$, $-3.29\%$ versus LSTM;
paired $t$-test $p<10^{-9}$, $d=-7.41$). A ten-seed
transaction-cost-inclusive rerun under $r=0.05$ confirms the
ordering ($-2.5\%$, paired $p=5.4\times 10^{-8}$). On a three-seed
\emph{walk-forward} backtest using raw \texttt{yfinance} OHLCV for
SPY and NIFTY-50 (train on calm 2017--2019, deploy on COVID-acute,
post-COVID and GFC windows; $n=1{,}150$ test windows; strictly
trailing features), RSE lowers CVaR$_{95}$ in both SPY crisis cells
on every seed ($-27\%$ COVID-acute, $-10\%$ GFC versus LSTM),
whereas under the $18$~bps NIFTY friction the low-turnover LSTM
baseline is best at the mean: the walk-forward evidence supports a
friction-conditional deployment rule rather than a uniform
architecture ranking.
Re-running the synthetic SPY/NIFTY stress battery with
full-fidelity models contracts the cross-architecture spread to
$\le 3\%$ per regime, so we frame W-DRO-T as a theoretically clean
Lipschitz regulariser rather than an empirical stress winner. All
three architectures admit a zero-cost rollback to a baseline and
add no inference-time cost.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} deep hedging, distributionally robust
optimisation, regime-switching ensemble, CVaR, walk-forward
backtest.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Hedging derivative claims under transaction costs, discrete
rebalancing and stochastic volatility is a central problem in
quantitative finance. The closed-form Black--Scholes
delta~\citep{black1973pricing} presumes continuous trading, zero
costs and a complete market; none of the three holds on a trading
desk. Deep hedging~\citep{buehler2019deep} responds by parameterising
the hedge as a neural network
$\delta_k = \delta_{\max}\tanh(F_\theta(I_{0:k},\delta_{k-1}))$ and
minimising a convex risk measure directly on the residual P\&L. The
framework has a strong empirical record on
stochastic-volatility models~\citep{horvath2021deep}, exotic and
long-dated insurance derivatives~\citep{carbonneau2021deep}, and
implied-volatility surfaces~\citep{imaki2021deep}. Three weaknesses,
however, recur once the policy leaves the calibrated training
environment.

Three weaknesses recur once the policy leaves its calibrated
training environment.
\emph{(i) Regime sensitivity}: single-calibration hedgers inflate
their tail risk mechanically when the volatility regime moves
(LSTM $\cvar_{95}$ on the SPY battery rises from $11.09$ at
$\sigma=15\%$ to $55.90$ at $\sigma=80\%$, a $5.04\times$ jump;
\S\ref{sec:spy}).
\emph{(ii) Curriculum instability}: the two-stage CVaR-then-entropic
protocol of~\citet{kozyra2019deep} switches losses abruptly; the
discontinuity can destroy the tail-aware solution acquired in the
first stage, and the standard fix---reducing the second-stage
learning rate---leaves the fine-tuning under-converged.
\emph{(iii) Regime-conditional optimality}: no single architecture
wins across friction regimes, and production desks ship a single
hedger anyway, leaving the regime-conditional choice on the table.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{W-DRO-T} (\S\ref{sec:wdrot}): a Wasserstein-DRO
causal Transformer whose gradient-norm penalty arises as the
first-order dual of a $\Wone$ ambiguity
ball~\citep{blanchet2019quantifying}. With full-fidelity
implementations the SPY stress battery contracts the
cross-architecture spread to $\le 1.4\%$ per regime
(Table~\ref{tab:spy}), so we keep W-DRO-T as a theoretically clean
Lipschitz regulariser with an auditable training-time penalty.
\item \textbf{3SCH} (\S\ref{sec:3sch}): a three-stage curriculum
LSTM whose homotopy loss
$\alpha\,\cvar_{0.95} + (1-\alpha)\,\rho_\lambda$ traces a
continuous solution path from a tail-aware optimum to the entropic
optimum; continuity follows from Berge's maximum theorem.
\item \textbf{RSE} (\S\ref{sec:rse}): a regime-switching ensemble
that gates frozen LSTM, Transformer and signature hedgers through
a learnable affinity matrix indexed by realised volatility, trend,
momentum and jump statistics. RSE wins the in-distribution
ten-seed benchmark ($\cvar_{95}=3.109\pm 0.010$, $-3.29\%$ vs.\
LSTM, $p<10^{-9}$, $d=-7.41$) and both SPY walk-forward crisis
cells on raw OHLCV on every training seed ($-27\%$ COVID-acute,
$-10\%$ GFC vs.\ LSTM across three seeds); under the $18$~bps
NIFTY friction the LSTM baseline stays ahead at the mean, so the
recommendation is friction-conditional.
\item \textbf{Unified evaluation pipeline}
(\S\ref{sec:experiments}--\S\ref{sec:walkforward}): ten-seed
in-distribution Heston, a $5{\times}7{\times}4$ extended suite, a
full-model regime-calibrated stress battery with SPY/NIFTY
parameters, a walk-forward OHLCV backtest, and a Basel-IV FRTB
capital decomposition.
\end{enumerate}

All three architectures admit zero-cost rollback to a baseline
($\varepsilon{=}0$, $T_2{=}0$, uniform gating) and add no
inference-time cost.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Deep hedging.}
\citet{buehler2019deep} parameterise the hedge as a neural network
and minimise a convex risk measure directly on the P\&L.
\citet{horvath2021deep} extend the framework to rough volatility,
\citet{carbonneau2021deep} to long-dated insurance derivatives, and
\citet{imaki2021deep} to volatility surfaces.
\citet{kozyra2019deep} introduces the two-stage CVaR-then-entropic
protocol that we adopt as the 3SCH baseline and that we generalise
through a continuous homotopy. None of these works addresses
distributional shift directly; they assume the training measure is
the test measure.

\paragraph{Distributionally robust optimisation.}
\citet{esfahani2018data} establish data-driven Wasserstein DRO with
finite-sample guarantees; \citet{blanchet2019quantifying} prove the
strong-duality theorem whose first-order expansion delivers the
gradient-norm penalty we use; \citet{gao2023distributionally} extend
the analysis to non-convex losses, which is the regime relevant to
neural-network policies. \citet{lutkebohmert2019robust} document
robustness gains during COVID-style shocks. Compared to these
works, our contribution is operational: we instantiate the
gradient-norm penalty inside a causal Transformer that respects
the non-anticipativity required by hedging, and we evaluate the
result on a market-calibrated battery rather than only on
synthetic stress scenarios.

\paragraph{Attention, signatures, ensembles, and RL.}
The Transformer~\citep{vaswani2017attention} and its financial
variants (Informer~\citep{zhou2021informer},
SigFormer~\citep{tong2023sigformer}) provide attention-based
sequence baselines. Path
signatures~\citep{lyons1998differential,kidger2020signatory} have
been used as static hedging features
by~\citet{cuchiero2020deep,abijaber2023hedging}; we include a
truncated-signature MLP as one of three RSE experts. Ensemble
variance reduction~\citep{dietterich2000ensemble} and
regime-switching models~\citep{hamilton1989new} motivate the RSE
gating; we adapt the mixture-of-experts paradigm by indexing
experts on continuous microstructure features rather than a
hidden Markov state, preserving differentiability of the gate.
Reinforcement-learning hedging~\citep{kolm2019dynamic,cao2021deep,neagu2025drl,huang2025sac}
is complementary: RL is preferable when the cost ledger is
path-dependent in a way that prevents an explicit P\&L objective.

\section{Problem Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Market dynamics}

We work on a filtered probability space
$(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_t)_{t\in[0,T]},\PP)$ supporting a
correlated Brownian motion $(W^S,W^v)$ with
$\langle W^S,W^v\rangle_t=\rho\, dt$ and $\rho\in(-1,0)$. The Heston
model~\citep{heston1993closed},
\begin{align}
dS_t &= rS_t\, dt + \sqrt{v_t}\,S_t\, dW_t^S, &
dv_t &= \kappa(\theta_v - v_t)\, dt + \xi\sqrt{v_t}\, dW_t^v,
\label{eq:heston}
\end{align}
is calibrated to four reference regimes (US-normal, post-COVID,
COVID and GFC-2008) and integrated by full-truncation
Euler--Maruyama. The reference single-asset calibration is given in
Table~\ref{tab:heston}. The Feller condition $2\kappa\theta_v>\xi^2$
holds for the reference parameters ($0.08>0.04$), so the variance
process is strictly positive in continuous time.

\subsection{Policy and P\&L}

At each step $k\in\{0,\dots,N\}$ the policy consumes the feature vector
$I_k=(S_k/S_0,\,\log(S_k/K),\,\sqrt{v_k},\,\tau_k,\,\Delta_k^{\mathrm{BS}})\in\R^5$,
where $\tau_k=(T-t_k)/T$ and $\Delta_k^{\mathrm{BS}}$ is the
Black--Scholes delta at the prevailing implied variance. The hedge
ratio is
$\delta_k=\delta_{\max}\tanh(F_\theta(I_{0:k},\delta_{k-1}))$ with
$\delta_{\max}=1.5$; the $\tanh$ bound regularises gradients without
forcing the unhedged corner. For payoff $Z=(S_T-K)^+$ and
proportional transaction cost $c_{\mathrm{tc}}=10^{-3}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{P\&L}(\delta) = -Z + \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\delta_k(S_{k+1}-S_k)
- c_{\mathrm{tc}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}|\delta_k - \delta_{k-1}|\,S_k,
\qquad \delta_{-1}=0.
\label{eq:pnl}
\end{equation}
The ledger charges the initial hedge purchase ($k=0$) and every
rebalance; the option is cash-settled at maturity and the terminal
liquidation trade is excluded, identically for every strategy, so
cross-model comparisons are unaffected. Training losses and all
reported evaluators implement exactly this convention.
RSE additionally consumes a six-dimensional regime vector
$r_k=(\mathrm{RV}_k^{(5)},\mathrm{RV}_k^{(10)},\mathrm{RV}_k^{(20)},
\mathrm{Trend}_k,\mathrm{Mom}_k,\mathrm{Jump}_k)$, combining
multi-window realised volatility, an SMA-crossover trend indicator,
log-return momentum and a bipower-variation jump
ratio~\citep{barndorffnielsen2002econometric}.

\subsection{Risk measures}

We work with the conditional value at risk and the entropic risk:
\begin{equation}
\cvar_\alpha(L) = \E[L\mid L\ge \VaR_\alpha(L)]
= \inf_{\nu\in\R}\Bigl\{\nu+\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}\E[(L-\nu)^+]\Bigr\},
\qquad
\rho_\lambda(X)=\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\log\E[\exp(-\lambda X)].
\label{eq:risks}
\end{equation}
CVaR is coherent~\citep{artzner1999coherent,rockafellar2000optimization};
the entropic risk is convex~\citep{follmer2002convex} and admits the
dual form
$\rho_\lambda(X)=\sup_{\QQ\ll\PP}\{\E_\QQ[-X]-\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\mathrm{KL}(\QQ\|\PP)\}$,
i.e.\ a KL ambiguity ball. We exploit two structural facts:
(i)~the KL ball requires $\QQ\ll\PP$ and therefore cannot place mass
on previously unseen volatility regions; (ii)~the Wasserstein ball
that we use in W-DRO-T does not require absolute continuity and so
can capture genuine support shift. Throughout we set $\alpha=0.95$
and $\lambda=1$.

\paragraph{Notation summary.}
$\delta_k$ is the hedge ratio at step $k$; $\Pi^\theta$ is the
terminal P\&L of policy $\theta$; $r_k$ is the regime feature vector;
$\rho$ denotes a convex risk measure (entropic risk $\rho_\lambda$
or $\cvar_\alpha$); $\Wone(\QQ,\PP)$ is the Wasserstein-1 distance
under Euclidean cost; $K$ is the number of regimes; $M$ is the
number of ensemble experts; $\varepsilon$ is the Wasserstein radius
and $\alpha$ is the homotopy coefficient.

\section{Three Architectures}\label{sec:methods}

The three architectures share the training driver of
\S\ref{sec:experiments}, the same $80$-epoch budget, the Adam
optimiser with weight decay $10^{-4}$ and gradient clipping at
$\|\nabla\|\le 5$. Architecture-specific modifications operate
strictly inside this budget.

\subsection{W-DRO-T: Wasserstein-DRO Transformer}\label{sec:wdrot}

\subsubsection*{Distributionally robust objective}

W-DRO-T targets the worst-case hedging problem
\begin{equation}
\theta^* \;=\; \argmin_\theta \sup_{\QQ:\,\Wone(\QQ,\PP)\le\varepsilon}
\E_\QQ\!\bigl[\rho\bigl(\mathrm{P\&L}(\delta^\theta)\bigr)\bigr]
\label{eq:dro_obj}
\end{equation}
over the Wasserstein ball
$\{\QQ:\Wone(\QQ,\PP)\le\varepsilon\}$. The Wasserstein ball admits
mass-transport between support points and so models support shift in
volatility, jump intensity and cross-asset correlation.

\begin{corollary}[Gradient-Penalty Approximation,
\citealp{blanchet2019quantifying}]\label{cor:grad_penalty}
For $\ell$ differentiable and locally Lipschitz under $\PP$,
\begin{equation}
\sup_{\Wone(\QQ,\PP)\le\varepsilon}\E_\QQ[\ell]
\;=\; \E_\PP[\ell] \;+\; \varepsilon\,\E_\PP\!\bigl[\|\nabla_X\ell\|_2\bigr]
\;+\; O(\varepsilon^2).
\label{eq:dro_loss}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\noindent\textit{Sketch.}
Strong duality gives
$\sup_{\Wone\le\varepsilon}\E_\QQ[\ell]
=\inf_{\lambda\ge 0}\bigl\{\lambda\varepsilon
+ \E_\PP[\sup_{x'}(\ell(x')-\lambda\|x-x'\|)]\bigr\}$.
A Taylor expansion of the inner $c$-transform at $x$ yields
$\sup_{x'}\{\ell(x')-\lambda\|x-x'\|\}
= \ell(x)+\tfrac{1}{4\lambda}\|\nabla_x\ell\|^2
+ O(\|\nabla\ell\|^4/\lambda^3)$.
Minimisation over $\lambda$ produces~\eqref{eq:dro_loss}.\hfill$\square$

The penalty $\varepsilon\,\E_\PP[\|\nabla_X\ell\|_2]$ is a Lipschitz
regulariser on the input--output map of the policy: it shrinks the
slope of $\ell$ in the directions in which the Wasserstein adversary
would push mass. Two consequences matter operationally. First, the
penalty is computed under $\PP$, so no extra simulation cost is
incurred; the cost is one additional backward pass per batch.
Second, at inference the regulariser is dropped: W-DRO-T evaluates
exactly as a vanilla Transformer.

\subsubsection*{Architecture}

A causal Transformer encoder with $d_{\mathrm{model}}=64$, $H=4$
heads, $L=3$ layers, $d_{\mathrm{ff}}=256$, dropout $0.1$ and
approximately $85$K trainable parameters. Sinusoidal positional
encoding and a strict causal mask enforce non-anticipativity; the
output head returns $\delta_k=\delta_{\max}\tanh(\cdot)$. Compared to
the LSTM baseline ($\sim 54$K parameters), the additional capacity
is modest and does not by itself explain the crisis-window gains.

\subsubsection*{Training}

Three phases (Algorithm~\ref{alg:wdrot}):
(i)~$\cvar_{0.95}$ pretraining for $50$~epochs at
$\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$;
(ii)~DRO-annealed entropic fine-tuning for $30$~epochs at
$\mathrm{lr}=10^{-4}$, with linear schedule
$\varepsilon_t=0.1\cdot t/30$ and the penalty
of~\eqref{eq:dro_loss}; second-order gradients
$\nabla_\theta\|\nabla_X\ell\|_2$ are computed via PyTorch
\texttt{create\_graph=True} and the math-SDPA backend;
(iii)~stress evaluation under shifted regime calibrations. The DRO
penalty roughly doubles Phase~(ii) wall time and adds zero
inference-time cost. Setting $\varepsilon\!\equiv\!0$ recovers a
vanilla Transformer.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{W-DRO-T training}\label{alg:wdrot}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Heston simulator $\PP$, batch size $B$, schedule $\{\varepsilon_t\}$
\STATE Initialise causal-Transformer $F_\theta$
\STATE \textbf{Phase 1 (CVaR pretraining, $50$ ep):}
\FOR{epoch $=1,\dots,50$}
  \STATE Draw $\{X^{(b)}\}_{b=1}^B\sim\PP$; compute $\Pi^\theta(X^{(b)})$
  \STATE $\mathcal{L}_1\gets \cvar_{0.95}(-\Pi^\theta)$;\quad Adam step at $\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Phase 2 (DRO-annealed entropic, $30$ ep):}
\FOR{$t=1,\dots,30$}
  \STATE Draw $\{X^{(b)}\}_{b=1}^B\sim\PP$; compute $\ell^{(b)}=\rho_\lambda(-\Pi^\theta(X^{(b)}))$
  \STATE $g^{(b)}\gets \nabla_{X^{(b)}}\ell^{(b)}$ \hfill (autograd, \texttt{create\_graph=True})
  \STATE $\mathcal{L}_2\gets \tfrac{1}{B}\sum_b\bigl(\ell^{(b)}+\varepsilon_t\,\|g^{(b)}\|_2\bigr)$
  \STATE Adam step at $\mathrm{lr}=10^{-4}$, $\|\nabla_\theta\|\le 5$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \RETURN $\theta^*$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{3SCH: Three-Stage Curriculum Hedger}\label{sec:3sch}

The two-stage CVaR-to-entropic switch of~\citet{kozyra2019deep} is
replaced by the homotopy
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha;\theta) =
\alpha\,\cvar_{0.95}(-\Pi^\theta) + (1-\alpha)\,\rho_\lambda(-\Pi^\theta),
\qquad \alpha:0.8\to 0.2.
\label{eq:homotopy}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Continuity of the 3SCH solution path]\label{prop:3sch}
For each $\alpha\in[0,1]$,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha;\cdot)$ is a convex risk measure,
and the solution correspondence
$\Theta^*(\alpha)=\argmin_\theta\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\alpha;\theta)$
is upper hemicontinuous on $[0,1]$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\textit{Sketch.}
Convexity, monotonicity and translation invariance are inherited
from the two components, so $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is a convex
risk measure for each $\alpha$. It is jointly continuous in
$(\alpha,\theta)$, and the parameter space is compact after weight
clipping; Berge's maximum theorem then yields upper
hemicontinuity~\citep{aliprantis2006infinite}.\hfill$\square$

\begin{remark}[Scope of Proposition~\ref{prop:3sch}]\label{rem:3sch_scope}
Upper hemicontinuity of the argmin correspondence does not by itself
guarantee that gradient descent traces a stable solution path in
parameter space; non-convexity of the network family leaves room for
basin transitions across $\alpha$. The proposition is intended as a
justification for the homotopy continuation idea; the operational
evidence that the path is stable is empirical and resides in the
in-distribution and cross-seed CV results of \S\ref{sec:results}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Architecture and budget.}
3SCH inherits the LSTM hedger ($2$~layers, hidden dimension $50$,
$\sim 54$K parameters) unchanged: any improvement is therefore
attributable to the schedule, not to capacity. The $80$-epoch
budget is partitioned into
Stage~1 ($T_1=40$, $\cvar_{0.95}$, $\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$), which
caches reference deltas $\delta^{\mathrm{ref}}$;
Stage~2 ($T_2=15$, $\alpha:0.8\to 0.2$,
$\mathrm{lr}=5\times 10^{-4}$); and
Stage~3 ($T_3=25$, $\rho_\lambda$ with a turnover penalty
$\gamma|\Delta\delta|$ and a no-trade band
$\nu\max(0,|\delta_k-\delta_k^{\mathrm{ref}}|-\epsilon)$).
The band term empirically prevents catastrophic forgetting of
tail-aware behaviour during the entropic stage. Setting
$T_2\!=\!0$ recovers the two-stage baseline
of~\citet{kozyra2019deep}.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{3SCH curriculum schedule}\label{alg:3sch}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $T_1=40$, $T_2=15$, $T_3=25$, schedule $\alpha_t:0.8\to0.2$
\STATE Initialise LSTM hedger $F_\theta$
\STATE \textbf{Stage 1 (CVaR, $T_1$ ep, lr $10^{-3}$):}
$\theta\leftarrow\arg\min\cvar_{0.95}(-\Pi^\theta)$; cache
$\delta^{\mathrm{ref}}=\delta^\theta$
\STATE \textbf{Stage 2 (homotopy, $T_2$ ep, lr $5\!\times\!10^{-4}$):}
\FOR{$t=1,\dots,T_2$}
  \STATE $\alpha_t\gets 0.8-0.6\,(t-1)/(T_2-1)$
  \STATE Step $\theta$ against
  $\alpha_t\,\cvar_{0.95}(-\Pi^\theta)+(1-\alpha_t)\,\rho_\lambda(-\Pi^\theta)$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Stage 3 (entropic, $T_3$ ep, lr $10^{-4}$):}
\FOR{$t=1,\dots,T_3$}
  \STATE Step $\theta$ against $\rho_\lambda(-\Pi^\theta)
  + \gamma|\Delta\delta|
  + \nu\,\max(0,|\delta-\delta^{\mathrm{ref}}|-\epsilon)$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \RETURN $\theta^*$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{RSE: Regime-Switching Ensemble}\label{sec:rse}

\subsubsection*{Gating}

RSE combines $M=3$ frozen base hedgers (LSTM, Transformer and a
truncated-signature MLP) under a regime-aware gate. At step $k$,
\begin{equation}
\delta_k^{\mathrm{RSE}} = \sum_{m=1}^M w_m(r_k)\,\delta_k^{(m)},\qquad
w_m(r_k) = \sum_{j=1}^K p(r_k=j)\,[\mathrm{softmax}(A/\tau)]_{j,m},
\label{eq:rse}
\end{equation}
where $K=4$ regimes, $A\in\R^{4\times 3}$ is a learnable affinity
matrix ($KM=12$ trainable scalars), and $p(r_k=\cdot)$ is the softmax
output of a three-layer MLP $f_\phi:\R^6\to\R^4$ on the regime
vector $r_k$ of \S\ref{sec:setup}. The regime probabilities, expert
weights and affinity matrix together expose a three-level audit
trail. Soft gating at $\tau=1$ avoids the discontinuous strategy
changes that hard switching would induce at regime boundaries (and
the spurious transaction costs they trigger).

\begin{proposition}[Variance reduction]\label{prop:vrf}
For an equal-weight ensemble of $M$ unbiased hedgers with average
pairwise correlation $\bar\rho$ and average variance $\bar\sigma^2$,
$\mathrm{Var}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ens}})=\bar\rho\bar\sigma^2 +
\tfrac{1-\bar\rho}{M}\bar\sigma^2$, so the variance reduction factor
is $\mathrm{VRF}=(1-\bar\rho)(M-1)/M$. For $M=3$ and $\bar\rho=0.6$,
$\mathrm{VRF}\approx 26.7\%$.
\end{proposition}

The minimum-variance regime-conditional weights
$w_m^*(r) = [\Sigma(r)^{-1}\mathbf{1}]_m /
(\mathbf{1}^\top\Sigma(r)^{-1}\mathbf{1})$ weakly dominate the
uniform allocation~\citep{dietterich2000ensemble}, which RSE
recovers when $w_m\!\equiv\!1/M$. The bound is tight only at the
assumed $\bar\rho$; we adopt $\bar\rho=0.6$ as an illustrative
value, and find the realised cross-expert error correlation lies
in $[0.55,0.7]$ across seeds in our experiments.

\subsubsection*{Training}

$30$~epochs of $\cvar_{0.95}$ per base model (frozen thereafter);
$20$~epochs of $\cvar_{0.95}$ on $(\phi,A)$ at
$\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$; $30$~epochs of entropic fine-tuning on
$(\phi,A)$ at $\mathrm{lr}=10^{-4}$. Total training budget is
$3\!\times\!30+20+30=140$~model-epochs, which is comparable to the
$80$-epoch baseline budget once the frozen-base reuse is amortised
across deployments. Inference latency is $<\!10$~ms per step on a
single GPU.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Three-layer benchmark.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A ten-seed in-distribution European-call benchmark on Heston
with $80$K paths ($50$K train, $10$K validation, $20$K test) and
seed grid $\{42,142,242,\dots,942\}$. Each seed controls data
generation, weight initialisation, batch ordering and dropout masks.
The headline CVaR$_{95}$ figures in Table~\ref{tab:main} were
generated with $r=0$; a TC-inclusive rerun under $r=0.05$ for the
LSTM-vs-RSE pair (Table~\ref{tab:tc_check}) confirms that the
relative ranking is invariant to both the rate change and the
inclusion of the explicit transaction-cost term in the evaluator.
\item A medium-scale extended suite of
$5\!\times\!7\!\times\!4=140$~cells: five hedgers $\times$ seven
payoffs (European call/put, up-out call, down-in put, digital,
Asian, basket-of-three) $\times$ four regime calibrations
(normal/post-COVID/COVID/GFC-2008), with two seeds per cell
($\{42,142\}$) and $115$~min wall time on Apple Silicon. The
medium suite uses the compact re-implementations of
\texttt{jaws\_research} rather than the full-fidelity models of
\S\ref{sec:methods}; its results are directional rather than
quantitative (see \S\ref{sec:results}).
\item A regime-calibrated stress battery on SPY and NIFTY-50
parameter scenarios: Heston simulators calibrated to the historical
realised volatility of four reference regimes (Table~\ref{tab:crisis_windows}),
$5{,}000$ test paths per cell, with $3$~bps (SPY) and $18$~bps
(NIFTY) proportional cost. The original numbers reported in this
battery used the simplified validation-grade models bundled in
\texttt{extended\_real\_market\_validation.py}; we rerun the same
scenarios with the full-fidelity model implementations from
\texttt{new\_approaches/code/} in Table~\ref{tab:realmarket_full}.
The training and test measures coincide within each cell, so the
battery tests model performance \emph{under} each regime rather
than \emph{across} them; the train-calm/deploy-crisis question is
answered by the walk-forward backtest on raw OHLCV of
\S\ref{sec:walkforward}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Statistical protocol.}
Bootstrap $95\%$ confidence intervals are computed from $10{,}000$
resamples on the ten-seed run and $2{,}000$ resamples on the medium
suite~\citep{efron1979bootstrap}. Paired $t$-tests are taken against
the LSTM baseline within each cell; normality is checked by
Shapiro--Wilk at $\alpha=0.05$ and Wilcoxon signed-rank is used as a
fallback. Multiple-testing correction is Holm--Bonferroni at
family-wise $\alpha=0.05$~\citep{holm1979simple}. Effect sizes are
Cohen's $d=(\mu_a-\mu_b)/\sigma_p$ with pooled standard
deviation~\citep{cohen1988statistical}.

\paragraph{Train/validation/test disjointness.}
All Heston path samples are drawn at experiment-launch with disjoint
seeds for the training, validation and test sets; the test paths are
not seen during gradient updates, early stopping, or hyperparameter
selection. Within the regime-calibrated stress battery, each cell
uses its own train / validation / test seeds; the calibration to
historical windows enters only through the choice of
$(v_0,\theta_v)$ in the Heston block.

\paragraph{Seed budget and scope.}
The ten-seed in-distribution benchmark is well powered: cross-seed
standard deviations are $\le 0.030$ and bootstrap intervals are
tight (\S\ref{sec:results}). The medium suite uses $n=2$--$3$ seeds
per cell; we treat its effect-size estimates as preliminary, report
them with that caveat, and use them to corroborate---rather than to
carry the quantitative weight of---the ten-seed and SPY rankings.

\paragraph{Compute.}
Training runs on either an NVIDIA GPU (PyTorch 2.0+/CUDA 11.8+) or
an Apple Silicon MPS device. The training driver is device-agnostic.
A single ten-seed European-call training pass takes $\sim\!5$~min
for LSTM/3SCH, $\sim\!15$~min for RSE (including base pretraining)
and $\sim\!2$~h for W-DRO-T on MPS (second-order gradients
dominate).

\subsection{Turnover--CVaR frontier}\label{sec:frontier}

Figure~\ref{fig:frontier} plots realised turnover against
$\cvar_{95}$ across all medium-suite cells. Two clusters emerge:
LSTM, 3SCH and RSE form a low-turnover, higher-CVaR cluster
(turnover $0.7$--$1.2$), while Transformer and W-DRO-T form a
high-turnover, lower-CVaR cluster (turnover $1.3$--$1.9$). The
implication is operational rather than purely statistical: the
preferred architecture is determined by the user's friction budget,
not by the in-distribution ranking alone. The break-even friction
$c^\star$ at which the high-turnover cluster ceases to be capital-
or P\&L-optimal can be read directly from the slope of the frontier;
in our SPY/NIFTY data this happens between $10$ and $15$~bps,
which is exactly the gap between US ($3$~bps) and Indian
($18$~bps) execution conditions.

above should be read as $\pm 0.1$ P\&L units. The remaining cells
were run on a single seed because of the wall-time budget and are
reported here without an explicit cross-seed dispersion estimate.

\subsection{Walk-forward backtest on raw OHLCV}\label{sec:walkforward}

We complement the synthetic stress battery with a walk-forward
backtest on raw \texttt{yfinance} OHLCV. Each model is trained on
overlapping $30$-day windows from the calm $2017$--$2019$ period
(SPY for the US battery; \texttt{\^{}NSEI} for the Indian battery)
and then evaluated, with frozen weights, on overlapping $30$-day
windows of three test periods: COVID-acute, post-COVID 2021, and
GFC 2007--2009. The policy consumes the same five Heston-style
features as the synthetic battery, with realised volatility
computed from a trailing $20$-day window of log-returns realised
strictly \emph{before} each decision time, so every feature is
measurable when the hedge is chosen. Each (market, model) pair is
trained at three seeds $\{42,142,242\}$;
Table~\ref{tab:walkforward} reports the TC-inclusive CVaR$_{95}$
as the cross-seed mean $\pm$ standard deviation.

\paragraph{Reading the walk-forward result.}
RSE improves on the LSTM baseline in both SPY crisis cells on
every training seed: $-24.5/-21.1/-35.0\%$ per seed on COVID-acute
(mean $-27.3\%$) and $-3.6/-8.8/-15.6\%$ on the GFC window (mean
$-9.7\%$). The SPY post-COVID 2021 window is a statistical tie:
the three architectures lie within $2.7\%$ of one another at the
mean and the per-seed winner alternates. The NIFTY ordering
reverses: LSTM is best at the mean in both cells ($+3.6\%$ and
$+6.3\%$ for RSE) and wins two of three seeds in each. The
mechanism is visible in the turnover ledger: on NIFTY, RSE turns
over $\approx\! 1.0$--$1.1$ units per window against
$\approx\! 0.67$--$0.74$ for LSTM, and at
$c_{\mathrm{tc}}=18$~bps the extra trading is exactly the drag
that erases the gating advantage; on SPY at $3$~bps the turnovers
are comparable ($1.06$ vs $1.07$ on COVID-acute) and the
crisis-window gains survive. The walk-forward battery therefore
gives out-of-sample support to the friction-conditional reading of
the turnover--CVaR frontier (\S\ref{sec:frontier}): below the
$10$--$15$~bps break-even, the regime-gated ensemble is the crisis
hedger of choice; above it, the low-turnover baseline is
preferred. Two caveats apply. First, the COVID cells contain only
$26$--$30$ overlapping windows, so their CVaR$_{95}$ rests on one
or two tail windows per seed; second, stride-one windows are
serially dependent, so cell values are descriptive statistics
rather than i.i.d.\ estimates. Out-of-distribution cross-seed
dispersion (CV up to $16\%$ on SPY COVID) is two orders of
magnitude larger than the in-distribution CVs of
Table~\ref{tab:realmarket_full}, which is why the seed-uniform
pairing, not in-distribution seed stability, is the robustness
statement this battery supports. Combined with the
in-distribution $-3.29\%$ headline of Table~\ref{tab:main}, the
walk-forward battery is the strongest empirical case for RSE in
low-friction crisis deployment---and it delimits, rather than
obscures, where that case does not extend.

\subsection{Transaction-cost-inclusive evaluation check}\label{sec:tc_check}

The headline in-distribution CVaR$_{95}$ values in
Table~\ref{tab:main} are evaluated on the unhedged-cost component
$-Z+\sum_k\delta_k(S_{k+1}-S_k)$, while the training loss operates
on the full P\&L formula~\eqref{eq:pnl} including the proportional
transaction-cost term. A closed-form correction
\[
\widehat{\cvar}^{\,\mathrm{tc}}_{95}
\;\approx\;\cvar_{95}^{\,\mathrm{no-tc}}
\;+\;c_{\mathrm{tc}}\,S_0\,\bar v,
\qquad c_{\mathrm{tc}}=10^{-3},\quad S_0=100,
\]
where $\bar v$ is the mean per-path turnover, recovers the
TC-inclusive ordering directly from Table~\ref{tab:main}
(Table~\ref{tab:tc_check}). RSE remains ahead of every baseline; the
gap relative to LSTM contracts from $-3.29\%$ to $-2.45\%$ because
RSE has higher turnover ($\bar v = 1.379$ versus $1.136$ for LSTM).
The relative ranking on the other three architectures is preserved.

\paragraph{Empirical verification.}
We retrained both LSTM and RSE under $r=0.05$ with the
TC-inclusive evaluator of~\eqref{eq:pnl} on the full ten-seed
grid $\{42,142,\dots,942\}$ (\texttt{full\_tc\_validation.py};

confirms both the closed-form prediction and the rank invariance:
\[

\hline

The two bootstrap intervals do not overlap. All three evidence

correction of Table~\ref{tab:tc_check}, and this ten-seed

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Deployment recipe.}
Two axes determine the architecture choice: regime stability and
transaction cost. In low-friction books ($\lesssim\!10$~bps) RSE
is the recommended default. It wins the in-distribution ten-seed
benchmark with a statistically significant margin ($p < 10^{-9}$,
Cohen's $d=-7.41$; \S\ref{sec:results}), improves both SPY
walk-forward crisis cells on every training seed ($-27\%$
COVID-acute, $-10\%$ GFC; \S\ref{sec:walkforward}), wins four of
seven cells in the full-model synthetic battery
(Table~\ref{tab:realmarket_full}), exposes an interpretable regime classifier for
governance, and has the smallest cross-seed coefficient of
variation ($0.30\%$). In high-friction books ($\sim\!18$~bps) the
low-turnover bias of 3SCH/LSTM becomes material: the break-even
friction visible in Figure~\ref{fig:frontier} (\S\ref{sec:frontier})
lies between $10$ and $15$~bps, and the NIFTY walk-forward cells
confirm the crossing out of sample---LSTM is the best NIFTY
crisis hedger at the mean while RSE carries roughly $1.5\times$
its turnover. Where computational budget or
governance constraints rule out the ensemble, LSTM and 3SCH are
near-substitutes with cross-architecture spread $\le 1.4\%$ per
regime in the synthetic battery.

\paragraph{Interpretability and governance.}
Each architecture exposes a distinct audit trail. W-DRO-T surfaces
$\|\nabla_X\ell\|_2$ as a Lipschitz proxy for input sensitivity,
which a risk officer can monitor during shadow-trading. 3SCH
inherits an unmodified LSTM, so the novelty is fully captured by
the documented schedule
$(\alpha_{\mathrm{start}},\alpha_{\mathrm{end}},T_1,T_2,T_3)$, and
any model-risk committee can evaluate the change without rereading
network weights. RSE exposes a three-level stack: time-varying
regime probabilities $p(r_k=j)$, time-varying expert weights
$w_m(r_k)$, and the static affinity matrix
$A\in\R^{4\times 3}$ (Table~\ref{tab:affinity}). Each architecture
admits a zero-cost rollback: $\varepsilon\!=\!0$ recovers a vanilla
Transformer, $T_2\!=\!0$ recovers two-stage training, and
$w_m\!\equiv\!1/M$ recovers an equal-weight ensemble. None of the
rollbacks requires retraining.

\paragraph{Wasserstein-DRO scope.}
The Wasserstein penalty acts as a Lipschitz regulariser on the
policy by constraining $\|\nabla_X\ell\|_2$ along directions
along which the adversary would transport mass. The full-model
SPY rerun (Table~\ref{tab:spy}, lower block) shows the penalty
does not yield a measurable CVaR advantage once LSTM, 3SCH and
RSE are themselves full-fidelity: at the present single-asset
dimension and an exact Heston simulator the four architectures
solve the same problem to within $\sim 1\%$. We expect
transport-direction smoothness to matter more for (i) larger
model classes than the $\sim 85$\,K-parameter Transformer used
here or (ii) genuinely multivariate payoffs; both are left to
future work. W-DRO-T is therefore retained as a theoretically
motivated Lipschitz penalty with auditable training-time cost,
not as an empirical stress-window winner.

\paragraph{3SCH at high friction.}
The Stage-3 turnover penalty $\gamma|\Delta\delta|$ and no-trade
band $\nu\max(0,|\delta-\delta^{\mathrm{ref}}|-\epsilon)$ push the
LSTM towards a low-turnover hedge while preserving its tail-aware
Stage-1 solution. At $3$~bps the cost component is negligible and
the lower tail accuracy of the Transformer dominates; at $18$~bps
the cost component dominates the P\&L, and the 3SCH bias becomes
correct. This is consistent with classical discrete-cost hedging
theory~\citep{leland1985option,whalley1997asymptotic}, in which
the optimal hedge has a no-trade band whose width is monotone in
the cost rate.

\paragraph{RSE in distribution vs.\ at stress.}
Ensemble variance reduction (Proposition~\ref{prop:vrf}) is
largest when expert errors are partially uncorrelated, which is
the regime RSE encounters in distribution. At high volatility the
three frozen experts move in the same direction and their
correlations rise towards unity, shrinking the variance
reduction. In the full-fidelity stress rerun
(Table~\ref{tab:realmarket_full}) RSE still wins four of seven
cells, but by a smaller margin: under the same regime calibration
the architectures are near-substitutes in CVaR$_{95}$, and the
gate contributes mainly through cross-seed stability and the
interpretable audit trail.

\paragraph{Limitations.}\label{sec:limitations}
\textit{Synthetic stress battery is within-regime}: cells train
and test on the same calibration; only the walk-forward battery
(\S\ref{sec:walkforward}) replays raw OHLCV.
\textit{Simplified-validator artefacts}: the original
Table~\ref{tab:realmarket} uses validation-grade counterparts of
the full models; the full-fidelity rerun
(Table~\ref{tab:realmarket_full}) shrinks the cross-architecture
spread to $\le 3\%$ and removes the $41/46/38\%$ W-DRO-T claim.
W-DRO-T is absent from the full-fidelity rerun due to per-cell
second-order wall-time constraints.
\textit{In-distribution evaluator}: Table~\ref{tab:main}
CVaR$_{95}$ omits the TC term that the training loss applies;
the closed-form correction
$\Delta\cvar_{95}\!\approx\!c_{\mathrm{tc}}\bar S_0\bar v$ and the
ten-seed rerun of \S\ref{sec:tc_check} both preserve the RSE-vs.-LSTM
ordering ($p<10^{-7}$).
\textit{Simulator coverage}: Heston omits flash crashes; Bates
adds jumps but no stochastic correlation; exotics are capped at
$d=3$.
\textit{W-DRO-T approximation}: first-order expansion in
$\varepsilon$ loses tightness for $\varepsilon\gtrsim 0.2$ and
requires the math-SDPA backend at training (inference is
unaffected).
\textit{Medium-suite power}: $n=2$ seeds per cell; results are
directional only.
\textit{Walk-forward dispersion}: three training seeds;
out-of-distribution cross-seed CV reaches $16\%$ on SPY COVID, and
the COVID cells contain only $26$--$30$ overlapping, serially
dependent windows, so cell-level CVaR$_{95}$ is a noisy
descriptor. The SPY crisis conclusion rests on the seed-uniform
pairing, and the NIFTY reversal shows the walk-forward ranking is
friction-dependent rather than universal.
\textit{Capital calibration}: absolute dollar figures depend on
$m_s$ and the $\ES_{97.5}/\cvar_{95}$ relationship; only the
ratios are robust.
\textit{3SCH theory}: Proposition~\ref{prop:3sch} gives upper
hemicontinuity of the argmin, not basin stability of SGD
(Remark~\ref{rem:3sch_scope}).

\paragraph{Future work.}
Streaming online updates of the RSE gating matrix; scaling to
$d\gtrsim 10$ assets with sparse attention; a 3SCH-DRO hybrid that
combines the curriculum schedule with the gradient-norm penalty;
wider walk-forward coverage (more markets, non-overlapping
windows, larger seed grids); rough-path signature
features~\citep{lyons1998differential,kidger2020signatory} for
non-Markovian dynamics.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented three architectural responses to documented
weaknesses of two-stage deep hedging---W-DRO-T (Wasserstein-DRO
Transformer), 3SCH (three-stage curriculum LSTM) and RSE
(regime-switching ensemble)---each grounded in distinct theory and
admitting a zero-cost rollback to a baseline. RSE attains the
lowest in-distribution tail risk on a ten-seed Heston benchmark
($-3.29\%$ vs.\ LSTM, $p<10^{-9}$); on a three-seed walk-forward
backtest on raw SPY and NIFTY-50 OHLCV ($n=1{,}150$ test windows)
it lowers crisis-window CVaR$_{95}$ on every seed in both SPY
cells ($-27\%$ COVID-acute, $-10\%$ GFC), while the $18$~bps
NIFTY cells favour the low-turnover LSTM baseline---out-of-sample
confirmation that the architecture choice is
friction-conditional. The full-fidelity rerun of the
synthetic SPY/NIFTY stress battery contracts the
cross-architecture spread to $\le 3\%$ per regime, so we frame
W-DRO-T as a theoretically clean Lipschitz regulariser rather
than as an empirical stress-window winner. The deployment recipe
is friction-conditional and is read off the turnover--CVaR
frontier of \S\ref{sec:frontier}.
